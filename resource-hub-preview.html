Why We Start Here

Everyone joins Blended for a different reason. You may want to build strength, improve your health, lose weight, train for an event, increase your energy, or simply feel more confident.

We do not believe in guessing where you should begin.

Your Success Session gives us the information we need to connect your goals to a realistic plan. It is not just about collecting numbers. It is about understanding why you joined and helping you use your first three months well.

What We Measure

The InBody 380 provides a detailed view of what is happening inside your body, beyond what a traditional scale can show.

  • Body weight
  • Skeletal muscle mass
  • Body fat mass
  • Body fat percentage
  • Lean body mass
  • Total body water
  • Segmental muscle analysis
  • Visceral fat level
  • Basal metabolic rate
  • InBody score

Blood Pressure

Your blood pressure gives us an important snapshot of your cardiovascular health and provides another meaningful marker that can improve through consistent training and healthy habits.

Grip Strength

Grip strength is a simple but valuable measurement of overall strength, function, and healthy aging. It gives us another baseline we can monitor as your training progresses.

Find Your Place

Your First 6 Weeks at Blended

You have already done one of the hardest parts. You walked through the doors. Your first six weeks are not about becoming the fittest person in the room. They are about building a routine, meeting people, and beginning to make Blended part of your life.

Weeks 1–3: Build the Habit

For your first three weeks, your main goal is simple:

Show up.

You do not need to understand all of the programming yet. You do not need to find the perfect shoes. You do not need to know every movement.

You simply need to begin using the membership you purchased and create the habit of coming through the doors.

The most successful members at Blended show up for themselves and begin placing themselves inside the community.

Find the People You Enjoy Training With

Try Different Class Times

Every class at Blended has its own personality. The 5:45 a.m. members are different from the 4:15 p.m. members. They are at different points in their day, they bring different energy, and every group develops its own rhythm.

If your schedule allows, try a variety of class times during your first few weeks. You may be surprised by where you feel most comfortable and who you naturally connect with.

Do Not Wait for Connection to Happen

Let People Get to Know You

Joining a new gym can feel exciting, intimidating, nerve-racking, or all three at the same time.

Introduce yourself to other members. Talk with your coaches before or after class. Let them learn your personality, your goals, and what brought you to Blended.

Connection works both ways. The more people get to know you, the faster Blended will begin to feel familiar.

Weeks 4–6: Build Momentum

Your next three weeks are about maintaining the habits you have already started.

Maybe you attended three times each week during your first three weeks. Now may be the time to see what a fourth day feels like.

Do not add more simply because you think you should. Add more when your schedule, energy, and recovery allow you to do it consistently.

Help Your Body Adapt

Support Your Recovery

It is normal to feel sore when you begin. You are using muscles in new ways and asking your body to adapt to a different style of training. Support your recovery by focusing on:

  • Drinking enough water
  • Eating enough food to fuel your training
  • Getting adequate protein
  • Prioritizing sleep
  • Taking recovery days when needed
  • Asking your coach when something does not feel right

Fuel, hydration, and rest will help your body recover more easily and allow you to keep showing up.

Every Workout Can Be Adjusted

Ask Your Coaches

In every class, you should ask for movement adjustments when you need them. That may be because of an injury, discomfort, limited mobility, or because you are still developing the strength required for a specific movement.

Your coaches are there to help. Use the knowledge stored in their brains.

Scaling is not failing. It is how we make sure every workout is appropriate for your body and your current abilities.

When We Say Jump In, We Mean It

Join the Community

When you signed up at Blended, you did not only choose us. We chose you to become part of this community with us.

You may not have noticed, but we were learning about you too. Are you someone who will add to the culture? Will you support the people around you? Will you help make the room better?

You made it here, so the answer was yes.

Now it is time to begin experiencing more of what Blended offers.

Now that you have been here for a few weeks, let us have fun while we train our bodies and become stronger together.

How to Measure Success

Do not measure your first six weeks only by the number on the scale or how much weight you can lift.

Ask yourself:

  • Have I created a routine?
  • Do I recognize familiar faces?
  • Do the coaches know me?
  • Do I feel comfortable asking questions?
  • Am I beginning to look forward to coming back?

If the answer is yes, you are exactly where you need to be.

Training at Blended

There's more than one way to get better.

Strength. Conditioning. Running. Olympic lifting. Personal coaching. Your training doesn't have to fit into one box, and neither do you. At Blended, you can build a week that challenges you, keeps things interesting, and gives you a reason to keep showing up.

What's the best way to train at Blended?
The way you'll keep doing.

We're all different. Different goals. Different backgrounds. Different schedules. Different things we genuinely enjoy doing. There isn't a perfect formula of exactly how many HYROX or Ground Zero classes you should take every week.

Try things. Find what you enjoy. Mix it up.

Having variety in our training helps us stay adaptable, and enjoying the way you train makes it a whole lot easier to keep showing up.

So… what should your week look like?

There isn't one perfect formula.

You are unique. Your training should be too.

Some people love lifting heavy. Some love getting sweaty. Some want to run. Some want to learn new skills. And plenty of us want a little bit of everything.

Instead of trying to build the “perfect” training week, start by asking yourself:

What do I enjoy?

You're much more likely to stay consistent with training you actually look forward to.

What do I want to get better at?

  • Want to get stronger? Spend some more time in Ground Zero.
  • Want to build your engine? Try HYROX.
  • Want to become a better runner? Meet us at FSTR.
  • Want more individualized attention? Personal Training might be your answer.
  • Want to improve your Olympic lifts? Explore The Simple Method.

Can I add some variety?

You don't have to choose one thing. In fact, we encourage you not to.

Nutrition + Recovery

Your training is only part of the equation.

What you do between sessions matters too. Eating enough, staying hydrated, sleeping well and giving your body what it needs to recover can help you get more from the work you're already doing.

Recovery doesn't have to become another full-time job.

Train. → Eat enough. → Drink water. → Sleep. → Give your body time to adapt. → Repeat.

You don't need to optimize every minute of your day.

Start with the basics. Do them consistently. Then get more specific when your goals, or your body, tell you it's time.

Food is fuel.

Training asks your body to do a lot. Nutrition gives it the raw materials to do that work, recover from it and come back ready to do it again.

Instead of chasing the perfect diet, focus on consistently giving your body enough of what it needs.

Protein

Protein provides the building blocks your body uses to repair and maintain muscle. Spread protein-containing foods throughout your day rather than thinking only about the meal immediately after training.

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates aren't something athletes need to be afraid of. They're an important source of energy for training, particularly when you're running, doing HYROX, lifting and completing higher-intensity work.

Enough Food

Training hard while consistently under-fuelling can make it harder to perform and recover. Your nutritional needs are individual. If you're unsure whether you're eating appropriately for your training and goals, that's a great reason to get individualized support.

Hydration

How much water do I need?

There isn't one perfect water target for everyone. Your size, body composition, training, sweat rate, weather and daily activity can all affect how much fluid you need.

At Blended, we can use a simple bodyweight-based estimate as a starting point.

More Muscle / Leaner Body Composition

Approximately 1 L per 50 lb of bodyweight

Muscle tissue contains substantially more water than body fat, so people carrying more lean tissue generally have more body water.

Think about cooking chicken. As it cooks, you can physically see water leaving the muscle tissue. Muscle contains a significant amount of water; adipose tissue contains much less.

Higher Body-Fat Composition

Approximately 1 L per 75 lb of bodyweight

Using total bodyweight alone can overestimate fluid needs when a greater proportion of bodyweight is adipose tissue, so this provides a more conservative starting estimate.

These are starting estimates, not individualized medical hydration prescriptions. Exercise duration, sweat rate, heat, medications, health conditions and other factors can change fluid needs.
